Play the piano bank as recorded, and check that it still is

The FreePats upright-piano-kw bank as a real sampled instrument: 88 keys from
66 recorded files, encoded straight from the WAVs and left alone.

Nothing in this pipeline does what the pack builder does. No trimming of
leading or trailing silence, no peak normalisation to a family target, no loop
points, no 1024-sample marker, no loudness target. Levels came out at
0.999 to 1.000 peak across all 66 source files, which is the bank's own
recording level.

The codec delay is measured rather than worked around. LAME writes its encoder
delay into an MP3 and Chrome hands it back as leading silence, so starting at
sample zero puts every hammer about 24 ms late. The player decodes each file
once, finds where the sound actually starts, and starts the source node there --
measured between 0.024 and 0.030 s depending on the file. No marker, and it
stays right if the encoder or the bitrate changes.

Key mapping is the bank's own SFZ, read with the repo's parser:
pitch_keycenter, lokey/hikey, and the vL/vH velocity layers in the filenames.
All 88 keys have both hammers. 110 of the 176 layers are covers by a take the
bank recorded at a neighbouring pitch, at -100 to +300 cents; that is in the
manifest rather than papered over, because on some keys a change of velocity
is a change of string and on others it is only a change of hammer.

Per note: a new AudioBufferSourceNode, loop off, detune 0, playbackRate 1 where
the take was recorded at that key, and no stop() call at all. Nothing sits
between the source and the speakers.

Three things the check found that were not expected, all now fixed:

D#1vH does not fetch. FreePats writes sharps with a '#', a '#' in a URL is a
fragment separator, and encodeURI leaves it alone because '#' is reserved, so
the browser asked for "/samples/D". Six of the bank's sixty-six files are named
that way. The path is encoded a segment at a time now.

The level assertion was decorative. It compared each render against the peak
declared in this instrument's own manifest, which the builder writes -- so a
normaliser moves the audio and the declared peak together and passes. It reads
the original WAVs now, and says so, and skips rather than pretending when they
are not on disk.

And it was only measuring half the bank: it rendered the layer a hard strike
picks, which is the vH file, and left every vL unrendered. All 176 layers are
rendered now.

Both mutations were confirmed to fail it: starting at 0 instead of the measured
onset puts all 88 keys 22-30 ms late, and peak-normalising every take to 0.45
in the builder -- exactly what the pack builder does -- flags all 66 takes at
1.0 -> 0.436.

The builder no longer deletes its own output directory. It used to, and the
directory is where the hand-written page lives, so running it once took the
page with it and the next check reported a decode failure that had nothing to
do with audio.
